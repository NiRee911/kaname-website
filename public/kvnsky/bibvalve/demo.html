Work sits in Awaiting Test for 107.2 hours on average. Testing itself takes 9.0. The queue in front of the step is twelve times the step.

The smallest experiment

Limit work in Awaiting Test to 3 items. No new role, no new meeting, no reorganisation. When the limit is reached, the next person to finish development picks up testing instead of starting something new. Reversible in a day.
falsified if, within 3 sprints: queue shortens AND flow efficiency stays flat -> the waiting relocated, it did not go throughput falls -> the limit starves the team rather than focusing it; remove it

Both outcomes are worth more than the experiment quietly succeeding, because both are answers.

Human decision. The run proposes one experiment and its falsification. It does not adopt it.

The team names problems and does not schedule them. Eighteen of twenty-eight were never brought to planning at all - not deprioritised, never presented. A different failure from noticing nothing, and arguably a worse one: the diagnosis already exists, written down, and stops there.
Which is why the experiment above matters less than what happens to it. A proposal that goes the way of the other 28 changes no number, and the next run finds the same constraint and writes the same report.

What the script refuses to decide

targets_the_constraint: null - whether any open item addresses the test queue is a question about text, not timestamps. The titles are handed out instead, and reading them is an ASSUMPTION shown next to the titles it came from.

On a freshly introduced type, expect a refusal. Below 12 improvements the close rate is not computed - one closed out of three is not 33 %, it is nothing. These figures come from a fixture with enough history to show the shape of the signal.
