Four of five dynamics signals are blind. Sentiment, retro follow-through, recurring themes, who speaks and who stops. A tool built on the issue tracker sees neither what management sees nor what the person in the room sees - and the catalogue says so instead of substituting something adjacent.

Work sits in Awaiting Test for 107.2 hours on average. Testing itself takes 9.0. Every instinct says bottleneck.

The Theory of Constraints test says otherwise. Exit rate against entry rate - dwell 137.2 → 106.5 → 95.9 → 82.1 h with arrivals flat at 60. Ratio 0.6: work leaves at least as fast as it arrives, so the step is not bounding throughput. Long dwell without a growing queue is a batching policy, and that is latency, not throughput.

The smallest experiment

Do not add a WIP limit. That is the lever for a demand constraint. On a stable batching queue it changes nothing at best, and starves the upstream steps at worst.

Ask why testing happens in batches. Shared environment, a nightly run, a policy nobody revisited - each has a different fix, and none of them is a WIP limit. The record shows the queue; it cannot say which of those it is.
this reading flips if: arrivals rise while dwell stays flat -> it becomes a demand constraint after all, and a WIP limit is the right lever the batching turns out deliberate and priced -> PRO-02 measures a decision, not a fault. That objection belongs in disputed.yaml

Both outcomes are worth more than the experiment quietly succeeding, because both are answers.

Human decision. The run recommends asking a question, not making a change. It does not ask it.

What the script refuses to decide

targets_the_constraint: null - whether any open item addresses the test queue is a question about text, not timestamps. The titles are handed out instead, and reading them is an ASSUMPTION shown next to the titles it came from.

On a freshly introduced type, expect a refusal. Below 12 improvements the close rate is not computed - one closed out of three is not a rate, it is nothing. These figures come from a fixture with enough history to show the shape of the signal.
