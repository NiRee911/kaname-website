Why the standard asks: data without a retention period is kept forever by default, which no legal basis supports.

PARTIAL2.0 exp · OBS-01 · MUST · R-01 severeTraffic has no alert; saturation pages nobody

Why the standard asks: un-paged degradation is discovered by the customer, not by the team.

PARTIAL2.0 exp · OBS-02 · MUST · R-03 severeTwo of four alarms have no runbook link

Why the standard asks: an alert without a response procedure moves the outage from detection to guesswork.

PARTIAL2.0 exp · OWN-04 · MUST · R-03 severeRunbook last reviewed 2024-11-03, about 21 months

Why the standard asks: a stale runbook is more dangerous than a missing one, because it is trusted.

Proposed controls - 11 in total, top ones shown

Every alert declares which golden signal it serves. One alarm has no classification, no runbook and no paging route - it falls through every control in the pack.

High-risk fields rest on legitimate interest, but no control requires a balancing test.

Retention and deletion are not checked against the Brazilian read replica.

The log scrub list is never reconciled against the personal-data inventory.

Documented procedures are required to exist and be fresh - never to have been exercised.

Nothing requires knowledge transfer when a team loses its engineers.

The audit never promotes its own proposal. It reports what it saw, and the standard's owner decides whether that becomes a rule.
