One record, three rooms

Where this goes, and what is actually running today.

How it works → Watch the demo → ← kvnsky

The same twenty sprints answer three different rooms: the team every two weeks, the ART every quarter, and the business once a year. What changes between them is the zoom, not the evidence.

Each room asks its question today and gets an answer from someone's confidence. The record already contains a better one. Below: what each room would get instead, what it costs to be wrong there, and - stated plainly - which of the three exists right now.

What horizon 2 would hand you · before the event, not during it

Product management sends four initiatives ranked by priority. You are not asking the team to estimate them yet - you are asking the record how far down that list this team's capacity actually reaches, so you walk into PI Planning with questions instead of a blank page.

pi-forecast.md preview of a proposed artifact · capacity figures are computed from the record, the initiative list is illustrative

BUILD capacity in this PI

Four development iterations. The IP iteration is not counted - planning commitment across all five is a 20 % overrun before anything starts.

50 %142 ptsof BUILD work
85 %122 ptsthe line for a commitment
95 %110 ptsthe safe floor

Total throughput at 85 % is 283 points. Only 43.2 % of it has historically reached BUILD - the rest went to RUN and TECH. The roadmap assumes the whole team; the record says otherwise, and that gap is the single most useful thing to know before the room fills up.

The list, walked in priority order

#InitiativeSizeBasisCumulativeVerdict
1Live ETA for dispatchers44 3 comparable epics44 commit
2Driver app refresh61 2 comparables, both overran105 commit
3Billing export rework33 1 comparable - wide138 stretch
4Partner API for 3PL carriers- no comparable in the record- unsized

Three fit, two are committable. Items 1 and 2 sit inside the 85 % line. Item 3 clears 50 % but not 85 %, which is exactly what SAFe already calls an uncommitted objective - it goes on the board as stretch, not as a promise. Item 4 is not "too big": it is unknown, and that is a different problem with a different fix.

What to take into the room

  • Item 3 is the first stretch. If the team wants to commit to it anyway - what comes off the list? Ask it before the room decides by drift.
  • Item 2 rests on two comparables and both overran. What was different about them, and is it different this time?
  • Item 4 has no comparable. Ask for a spike before it carries a rank - a priority on an unsized item is a decision made without information.
  • Billing export touches the component with the highest carryover in the record. That is a dependency you can raise now, not on day two of the event.
What this cannot see, and does not pretend to: next quarter's absences, a reorg, or the real scope of anything product management has not yet described. And it does not replace the team - PI Planning still decides. This is a challenge pack, not a plan.

What horizon 3 would hand you · less detail on purpose

The same walk, four PIs out, and only the large initiatives. The further out, the coarser - and the label says which of them is a forecast and which is only a scenario.

roadmap.md preview of a proposed artifact · illustrative
PI-4 · forecastLive ETADriver app Billing export - stretch
PI-5 · forecastPartner API carry-in from PI-4, if any
PI-6 · scenarioDepot planning one large initiative, not two
PI-7 · beyond the record The team's history does not reach this far. Anything placed here is a wish with a border around it.

Two things the annual view adds that the quarter does not need: buffers sized from this team's measured variability instead of a flat percentage added at the end, and the seasonality already in the record - the weeks this team is reliably smaller, which surprises the roadmap every single year.

The honest part is the last band. A roadmap that fades out where the evidence ends is more useful than one that runs confidently to December, because the reader can see which promises are load-bearing.

Why the order matters · the cost of a wrong answer, by room

One sprintvisible, recoverable, nobody is surprised
One quarterplus every dependency drawn on it
One yearplus the credibility of the people who presented it

The effort to answer well is roughly the same in all three rooms - it is the same record, read at a different zoom. The cost of answering badly is not. That asymmetry is the whole case for doing this at all, and it is also why horizon one is built first: it is the cheapest place to find out the method is wrong.

What holds at every horizon

Never one number

A single figure becomes a commitment within a week and a measuring stick shortly after. Every answer carries its confidence.

The model never computes

Simulation runs in a script with a fixed seed. The model writes the sentences and none of the numbers.

Refusal is an output

Too little history, no forecast - stated as such. A tool that answers every team is a tool whose answers mean nothing.

Nothing about a person

No individual velocity, no ranking, in any artifact. Capacity is a property of a system.

What exists today, without rounding up:
  • Horizon 1 is running. A script with no dependencies and a self-check, verified on two teams - one that gets a forecast and one that is refused. Every number in its output traces to a single JSON file.
  • Horizons 2 and 3 are the shape, not the build. They stand on the same extract, and the order is a data dependency rather than a roadmap preference: the quarter needs the sprint record, the year needs the quarter's patterns.
  • Nothing here has touched a real system. The demo runs on a synthetic fixture whose shape copies a Jira export and whose content is invented.
